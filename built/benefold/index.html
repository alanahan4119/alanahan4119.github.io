Case study 04 · Product discovery & strategy

Benefold

Helping employees choose the health plan that fits the year they'll actually have.

Founder & product lead · Benefits technology · Live demo launched November 20, 2026

The problem

Every open enrollment, employees pick a health plan from a guide written for compliance, not for decisions. Many re-enroll in last year's plan or choose the lowest premium, and the plan that looks cheapest is often the most expensive one for the year they're about to have. Large employers solve this with decision-support tools built into their benefits platforms. Small and mid-size employers, who mostly work through brokers, usually have nothing.

Why I built Benefold

I came up with Benefold and set its direction: brokers and employers pay, not employees, because people use a tool like this once a year. A broker sets up an employer in minutes from the enrollment guide they already have, and employees open a company link and get a priced-out comparison in about five minutes, with no account. I used AI to help with market research and to build the product, and led decisions on the workflow, scope, pricing direction, and quality.

Discovery that changed the plan

I mapped the enrollment platforms and decision-support tools in the market, including bswift, Employee Navigator, PlanSource, ALEX, and Nayya. When I looked at pivoting into enrollment, I decided against it: it would have made Benefold the system of record for coverage, with carrier data feeds and full health-data obligations, in a market that is consolidating quickly. The gaps worth filling were around enrollment instead: getting plan data in at renewal, and decision support for the small groups that larger platforms don't serve.

What shipped

The demo works from both sides with fictional data. Brokers upload the plan summaries or an enrollment guide, and Benefold reads every medical plan out of them, premiums included, for the broker to review before anything goes live. They then share a link or QR code and track completions. Employees choose who's covered, answer about ten count-based questions, and see every plan ranked by expected yearly cost, with the worst case and what would change the answer.

A product decision that mattered

Benefold never stores what employees tell it. The cost math runs in the employee's browser, so survey answers, coverage choices, and results are never saved or sent anywhere. Employers and brokers see one number: how many people finished. That removed the biggest risk in the product, holding health information, instead of managing it, and it made the privacy promise simple enough to say in one sentence.

What a real enrollment guide taught me

Testing against a real employer's guide surfaced gaps the automated tests couldn't. The PPOs charged a flat $60 to $100 per pregnancy, which the first cost model didn't handle; it overstated costs by thousands for anyone expecting a baby. The document reader also read "100% after deductible" as a $0 copay. Both are fixed, and every fix now has a test that keeps it fixed.

Status and next steps

The demo went live on November 20, 2026. Next, I'm validating it with benefits brokers: whether they'd pay a small per-employee fee, how they get plan data in today, and whether they'd use it alongside the enrollment platform they already have.
